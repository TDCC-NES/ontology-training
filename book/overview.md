# Course overview

## Who this is for

This training is for researchers, PhD candidates, and research support staff (for example data stewards) who want to represent their domain knowledge as an ontology, so their data becomes more interoperable, reusable, and machine-actionable. It was developed for the Dutch Natural & Engineering Sciences research community, but the content applies to anyone who wants to learn ontology engineering with OWL 2 and linked data, regardless of institution or domain.

## Prerequisites

No prior experience with ontologies, RDF, OWL, or the Semantic Web is required, and no programming experience is required either. You don't need a use case of your own to get started: a shared running example is used throughout, though you're encouraged to apply what you learn to a domain of your own if you have one. All tools used in the training, such as Protégé, are introduced and installed as you go, so no advance setup is needed.

## What you'll learn

By the end of this training, you will be able to:

1. Explain what an ontology is, how it differs from a controlled vocabulary or a taxonomy, and why representing meaning in data instead of in code matters for interoperability, reuse, and integration.
2. Describe the core concepts of linked data, RDF, and OWL 2, and relate them to the FAIR principles.
3. Turn a real-world use case into competency questions and a shared conceptualization as the basis for an ontology design.
4. Build an OWL 2 ontology in Protégé, including classes, properties, domains, ranges, datatypes, and axioms, following good practice for IRIs, labels, and definitions.
5. Validate an ontology using a reasoner, SHACL, and manual checks, and fix common modeling problems.
6. Publish and document an ontology, and choose a license and versioning approach that fits your project.
7. Map an ontology to relevant external ontologies and vocabularies, and weigh the trade-offs between mapping approaches.
8. Connect real data to an ontology, and query, visualise, and reason over the result.
