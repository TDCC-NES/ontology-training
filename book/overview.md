# Course overview

## Who this is for

This training is for researchers, PhD candidates, and research support staff (for example data stewards) who want to represent their domain knowledge as an ontology to help make their data more interoperable, reusable, and machine-actionable. It was developed for the Dutch Natural & Engineering Sciences research community, but the content applies to anyone who wants to learn ontology engineering with OWL 2 and linked data, regardless of institution or domain.

## Prerequisites

No prior experience with ontologies, RDF, OWL, the Semantic Web or programming is required. You do not need a use case of your own to get started: a shared running example is used throughout.

For the practical chapters, you need a computer with a Web browser, a plain-text editor and permission to install applications. Check the setup instructions for the {doc}`Protégé walkthrough <curriculum/build-your-ontology-in-owl>`, {ref}`data conversion <rmlmapper-setup>` and {ref}`documentation generation <owl-ontology-structure>` before starting those chapters.

## What you'll learn

By the end of this training, you will be able to:

1. Explain what an ontology is, compare it with controlled vocabularies and taxonomies, and assess its value for describing, publishing, finding, integrating and reusing research data.
2. Explain the relationships and values expressed in a simple RDF example written in Turtle. Describe how Linked Data and OWL 2 can help applications interpret data consistently and support FAIR research data.
3. Define a research purpose and scope, formulate competency questions (questions the ontology should help answer), and sketch a shared model of relevant concepts and relationships.
4. Build a small OWL 2 ontology with classes, individuals, properties, domains, ranges, datatypes and logical axioms, using consistent IRIs, labels and definitions.
5. Check logical consistency with a reasoner, validate RDF data with SHACL, and use representative records to identify and correct modelling problems.
6. Document and prepare an ontology for publication, choose a licence and versioning approach that fits your project, and plan how it will be maintained.
7. Assess existing ontologies and vocabularies, reuse suitable terms, map your ontology to them, and justify your reuse and mapping choices.
8. Transform source records into RDF using an ontology, query the data with SPARQL, visualise its relationships and interpret inferred statements.

(how-to-use)=
## How to use this training

Browse all chapters, sections and subsections in the {ref}`course contents <course-contents>`.

Work through the curriculum in order, following the examples and walkthroughs. Each chapter builds on the last.

Publishing your own ontology is follow-up work, as explained in {doc}`chapter 7 <curriculum/publish-your-ontology>`.

In this training, we first build a small ontology to understand the modelling choices involved, then explore reuse in depth.

Found an error, or have a suggestion? Use the {octicon}`pencil;1em` edit button at the top of any page to propose a change on GitHub, or [open an issue](https://github.com/TDCC-NES/ontology-training/issues).
